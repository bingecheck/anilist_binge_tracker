How it works and what it can’t see
  • Every time you update an episode on AniList, it logs an activity with a timestamp. This page pulls that public history and finds, for each show, the biggest burst of episodes with no break longer than 3 days.
  • A show counts as binged when that burst holds at least 60% of the episodes you watched at 2 or more episodes a day. If you logged an episode at least 14 days before the burst started, it’s “waited, then binged”: the watch-episode-1-on-air, finish-it-later habit that start and finish dates hide.
  • Back-to-back runs are sequels (movies and specials included) that you started within 3 days of finishing the previous entry. A paused or dropped sequel started within 30 days still ends the run, which is how burnout shows up.
  • Slow watches never count against a show. Plenty of reasons to watch slowly have nothing to do with liking it.
  • It can’t see anything added through an import, or from before you started logging on AniList. Episodes logged in a single update are listed separately, since a real binge and catching up on your list look identical there.
  • Everything runs in your browser. Your data is fetched straight from AniList’s public API and cached only in this browser.
